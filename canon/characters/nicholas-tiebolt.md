# Historical identity and background

- Early narratives preserve his surname as **Tbalt** (Dimsdale) and **Tiebalt** (Langford); **Tiebolt** is the project's canonical spelling. **Thiebalt** remains a documented research variant. Diebold and Theobald have been searched as possible phonetic or clerical variants but are not established aliases.
- Langford calls him a “young German.” Dimsdale's account preserves “the Dutchman” as the camp label used for him. These labels support a German-speaking or German-associated background but do not establish a country, province, hometown, family, or immigration date.
- By author decision, the novel may treat Tiebolt as having been in Washington Gulch, Colorado, around 1861. The basis is Dimsdale's report that Tom Baume recognized a knife he had lent Tiebolt there two years before, in the presence of John X. Beidler and William Clark.
- Contemporary Colorado records independently establish Beidler's work on the Washington Gulch freight route and his May 1863 departure toward Bannack. They do not independently establish Tiebolt's presence, the loan, or the identities of Baume and Clark.
- Langford says Tiebolt left Nevada to retrieve the mules at Dempsey's ranch. Nevada City is therefore his probable working base in Alder Gulch, not a proven legal domicile.
- Dempsey's ranch and the Pas-sam-a-ri/Stinkingwater Valley are documented destinations or event locations, not established residences. No consulted source establishes residence in Virginia City or Bannack.
- The record does not establish a direct move from Washington Gulch to Nevada City. Any intervening camps, occupations, residences, or migration route remain unknown.
- The two 1846 New York passenger-list candidates examined during research are not accepted as Tiebolt. Later record searches did not produce a defensible identity chain to the Montana victim, and one result depended on a manifest-indexing error.
