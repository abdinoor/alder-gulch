# Locations

Record canonical geography, spatial relationships, physical descriptions, and scene-specific changes.

## Alder Gulch

- A roughly fourteen-mile gold-bearing gulch running toward the Ruby Valley in the Tobacco Root country.
- The placer discovery in May 1863 produced a rapid chain of camps and settlements sometimes called Fourteen-mile City.
- In December 1863 the gulch remained part of Idaho Territory; Montana Territory had not yet been created.
- The opening establishes its governing physical contrast: immense winter beauty and distance surrounding concentrated wealth with little effective institutional protection.

## Virginia City and Nevada City

- Virginia City is the largest settlement in the gulch at the time of the opening.
- Nevada City lies down-gulch to the northwest of Virginia City.
- Langford says Tiebolt left Nevada to retrieve the mules at Dempsey's ranch and later describes the mule he rode from Nevada. Treat Nevada City as Tiebolt's probable immediate working base, while preserving that the source does not prove formal domicile.

## Washington Gulch, Colorado

- Dimsdale reports that Tom Baume had lent Tiebolt a knife in Washington Gulch, Colorado, two years before Tiebolt's body was identified in December 1863; John X. Beidler and William Clark were reportedly present.
- By author decision, Tiebolt may be treated as having been there around 1861. The duration and character of his stay, the exact Colorado mining district meant by “Washington Gulch,” and his route afterward remain unverified.
- A May 1863 Colorado newspaper independently reports that X. Beidler had freighted to California Gulch and Washington Gulch during the preceding year or two before leaving for Bannack. This corroborates Beidler's use of the route, not Tiebolt's presence or the knife loan.

## Colorado mining corridor associated with Beidler

- The 1861 territorial election places J. X. Beidler at Georgia Gulch. A January 1862 newspaper places his pack train between Montgomery City and Denver. A May 1863 report connects his freighting work to California Gulch and Washington Gulch and records his departure toward Bannack.
- Sacramento Flats/Sacramento City belonged to the California Gulch mining region. A William Clark appears there as a retail dealer in the September 1862 tax list, but this is only an unresolved candidate for Dimsdale's Clark.
- Boulder City, Gold Hill, Central City, and Denver supply Baume/Baum candidate records. They must not be treated as one man's itinerary because the 1860 census records separate men named Thomas Baume and Thomas Baum.

## Dempsey's ranch and the Tiebolt route

- Early accounts say Tiebolt left Nevada City for Dempsey's ranch to retrieve mules after receiving payment for them.
- Dempsey's ranch was a destination on the Virginia City–Bannack road, not a known Tiebolt residence. His association with the route does not establish residence in either Virginia City or Bannack.
- The Pas-sam-a-ri or Stinkingwater Valley is associated with the discovery of Tiebolt's body, not with a known residence.
- The exact route and site of the approved pursuit remain to be verified; Chapter 1 treats the chase geography as reconstruction rather than surveyed fact.
