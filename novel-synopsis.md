# Alder Gulch — Synopsis

In the winter of 1863, the gold camps of Montana Territory are rich, isolated, and nearly lawless. Travelers disappear, gold shipments are stolen, and the few officials charged with keeping order lack the institutions necessary to protect witnesses, jurors, or even themselves.

When young Nicholas Tiebolt is murdered, suspicion falls on George Ives, an alleged road agent with dangerous associates. The people of Nevada City construct a courtroom in the street and attempt something extraordinary: a genuine public trial, complete with lawyers, witnesses, contested evidence, and the possibility of acquittal. But while Ives’s guilt is debated before the crowd, another judgment is being reached in private. Even if the trial works, the community cannot safely repeat it for every suspected outlaw.

Ives is convicted and hanged, but his execution does not settle the deeper crisis. Days later, prominent citizens organize a Vigilance Committee—an institution designed to provide the security formal law cannot. As the vigilantes pursue suspected road agents, extract confessions, and uncover what they believe is a criminal network reaching all the way to Sheriff Henry Plummer, their standard of justice begins to change. The question is no longer whether a man’s guilt can be proved beyond doubt, but whether the community can risk leaving him alive.

Set against the beauty and isolation of the Montana frontier, *Alder Gulch* traces the birth of order from fear, necessity, and uncertain evidence. It is a story about ordinary men trying to build justice before they possess a functioning state—and about how easily the determination to defend civilization can become permission to abandon its restraints.
