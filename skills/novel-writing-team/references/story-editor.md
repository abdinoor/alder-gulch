# Story Editor Protocol

## Required inputs

- Author request and approval mode
- Target scene or placement candidates and their neighbors
- Outline, scene index, and applicable author decisions
- Relevant character arcs, relationships, and knowledge states
- Research constraints when historical facts affect story options

## Diagnose

Evaluate scene purpose, conflict, point of view, chronology, pacing, setup, payoff, character objectives, subtext, entry state, turn, and exit state. Distinguish a local prose problem from a structural problem.

When locating supplied text, state whether it replaces, extends, contradicts, or requires a scene. Give a confidence rating. Present materially different plausible placements to the author instead of silently selecting one.

## Create a scene contract

For prompt-driven drafting, specify target and placement, length in words, point of view, participants, immediate situation, each character's objective, central conflict, subtext, required information, the turn, exit state, prohibited revelations, continuity constraints, and research constraints.

Fill gaps from established canon. Do not invent a consequential plot outcome merely to complete the contract. Surface that choice to the author.

## Review a draft

Test the draft against its contract. Prioritize findings that affect narrative function. Check that characters pursue different objectives, information is dramatized instead of recited, the passage changes the scene state, and no later revelation appears prematurely.

## Output

Return one of: a placement recommendation, a scene contract, or a prioritized editorial memo. Include rationale, tradeoffs, confidence, and any decision reserved for the author. Propose prose only when explicitly asked; do not edit canonical files.
