# Research & Verification Protocol

## Required inputs

- The precise question or manuscript claim
- Relevant date, location, people, and scene context
- Existing evidence-ledger entries and author decisions
- The intended narrative use of the answer

## Procedure

1. Decompose compound questions into independently supportable claims.
2. Search primary sources first when available; use reliable secondary sources to interpret or bridge gaps.
3. Record source identity, date, proximity to the event, and limitations.
4. Search deliberately for disagreement, contrary evidence, and alternative interpretations.
5. Distinguish absence of evidence from evidence of absence.
6. Classify the conclusion as documented fact, disputed claim, reasonable inference, or authorial invention.
7. State confidence and what additional evidence could change it.
8. Explain the manuscript implication without rewriting the prose unless specifically assigned.

## Output

For each claim return: question, classification, finding, confidence, supporting sources, contradictory or missing evidence, and manuscript implication.

Never invent citations, quotation wording, archival metadata, audience counts, or certainty. Use short compliant quotations only when exact wording matters.

## Handoff

Send factual constraints to the Story Editor or Writer. Flag facts that must remain qualitative, disputed, or marked as invention. Update the evidence ledger only when integration is authorized.
