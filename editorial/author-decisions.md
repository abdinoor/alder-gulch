## 2026-08-15 — Tiebolt's provisional pre-Alder Gulch history

- **Issue:** Whether the novel may treat Dimsdale's Washington Gulch report as part of Tiebolt's biography despite the lack of independent corroboration, and which other locations count as residences.
- **Decision:** Assume for story planning that Tiebolt was in Washington Gulch, Colorado, around 1861. Treat Nevada City as his probable working base when he left for Dempsey's ranch in 1863. Do not infer a direct Washington Gulch-to-Nevada City migration, residence at Dempsey's ranch, Virginia City, or Bannack, or a precise European birthplace. The two 1846 New York passenger candidates are excluded from Tiebolt's canon unless new linking evidence appears.
- **Rationale:** Dimsdale supplies a specific remembered object, place, approximate date, and witnesses; Langford twice identifies Nevada as Tiebolt's point of departure. Preserving the distinction between accepted premise, reasonable inference, event geography, and rejected identity candidates gives Tiebolt usable human history without manufacturing a genealogy or migration route.
- **Affected files:** `canon/characters/nicholas-tiebolt.md`, `canon/locations.md`, `canon/timeline.yaml`, `planning/cast.md`, `research/evidence-ledger.md`, `research/open-questions.md`. No manuscript prose is approved or changed by this decision.

## 2026-08-12 — Murder-first opening and evidentiary boundary

- **Issue:** Whether to begin with the Ives trial or with Tiebolt's final ride, and how directly to depict Ives and an alleged road-agent network.
- **Decision:** Open with Alder Gulch's landscape and frontier conditions, then an approved reconstruction of Tiebolt fleeing, Ives pursuing and overtaking him, and Tiebolt being left barely alive. Establish Ives as personally dangerous and tease his associations, while explicitly reserving judgment on whether those associations formed the organized criminal network described by later accounts. Withhold the gruesome killing details for the trial proceedings.
- **Rationale:** The opening should establish the landscape, gold rush, institutional vacuum, jeopardy, and justified demand for justice while preserving the novel's later inquiry into evidence and historical certainty.
- **Affected files:** `manuscript/01.01-the-chase.md`, `planning/scene-index.yaml`, `canon/characters/nicholas-tiebolt.md`, `canon/characters/george-ives.md`, `canon/timeline.yaml`, `canon/locations.md`, `research/evidence-ledger.md`, `research/invention-register.md`.

## 2026-08-13 — Chapter II focal cast and deferred chronicler

- **Issue:** Which historical figures should carry Chapter II's lived portrait of the gulch, and when to introduce the two principal pro-vigilante chroniclers.
- **Decision:** Develop Henry Edgar as a leading Chapter II focal candidate because his discovery experience and diary continue the Chapter I strike thread. Use a named woman and family rather than generic vulnerability; the Sheehan family is the leading choice, with Anne as an adult household and economic presence and Mary as a curious, active child and later memoirist. Thomas Dimsdale is the stronger Chapter II chronicler because he connects early schooling and community formation to the first influential published Vigilante history. Retain Nathaniel Pitt Langford in the cast but defer his introduction, possibly until Part V, where he can represent the later preservation and enlargement of the Vigilante story.
- **Rationale:** These choices make the gulch concrete through discovery, domestic economy, childhood, education, and memory while preventing Chapter II from becoming a roll call of later-famous men. Delaying Langford keeps his retrospective function distinct from Dimsdale's immediate community role.
- **Affected files:** `planning/cast.md`; future Chapter II planning and character records when approved.

## 2026-08-13 — Williams and Beidler narrative division

- **Issue:** Whether Captain James Williams or John X. Beidler should carry the operational Vigilante storyline and the reader's initial attraction to extralegal action.
- **Decision:** Use Williams as a principal through-line character, introduced during the Tiebolt investigation and developed into the Vigilantes' operational leader. His proximity to the physical consequences of Tiebolt's killing should initially align the reader with his desire for decisive action, while the Ives trial exposes the difference between what he directly knows and what he can infer or prove. Williams carries continuity from the aftermath of the crime through investigation, trial, Vigilante formation, and field operations. Use Beidler as a sharp secondary presence, introduced at the Ives trial and deployed selectively when impatience with procedural restraint hardens into lethal enforcement.
- **Rationale:** Williams can sustain the book's principal movement from evidence to uncertainty to organized force, linking the public inquiry into provable guilt with the private turn toward preventive security. Beidler is more effective in concentrated appearances and should not absorb the collective responsibility of respectable organizers, field commanders, and community supporters.
- **Affected files:** `planning/cast.md`; future Williams and Beidler character records and scene planning after factual verification.

## 2026-08-13 — Chapter II chronology and Edgar opening

- **Issue:** The chronological range of “The Gulch” and which discoverer anchors its opening.
- **Decision:** Chapter II opens with Henry Edgar and the May 1863 discovery of gold in Alder Gulch, then advances through the rush, settlement, and emerging community to the December 1863 narrative present. Nathaniel Pitt Langford remains deferred and is not introduced through the discovery sequence.
- **Rationale:** Edgar provides a documented participant thread from the consequential pan to the human transformation of the gulch, while the May-to-December frame lets the chapter dramatize a place becoming a provisional society within months.
- **Affected files:** `structure-outline.md`, `planning/cast.md`; future Chapter II scene contract and scene index entry.

## 2026-08-13 — Adopt bounded author voiceprint

- **Status:** Superseded later on 2026-08-13 by “Lock narrator voice and supersede experimental voiceprints.”
- **Issue:** Whether and how to derive novel-writing guidance from an approximately 9,700-word corpus consisting mainly of scholastic exposition rather than fiction.
- **Decision:** Adopt the corpus's well-supported underlying habits—structure before ornament, concrete instances, contrast and legitimate exception, clear causality, qualified judgment, functional repetition, and plain declarative emphasis—as authorial guidance. Do not treat school-era diction, grammar, transitions, assignment structure, or explanatory cadence as a fictional style to reproduce. Keep narrative distance, imagery, dialogue, interiority, humor, and mature sentence music provisional until supported by creative-writing exemplars.
- **Rationale:** The corpus reliably shows how the author organizes attention and tests claims even though it cannot establish a complete fiction voice. A bounded profile makes those durable instincts usable without overfitting the novel to scholastic surface features.
- **Affected files:** `canon/style-and-voice.md`, `editorial/proposals/author-voiceprint.proposed.md`, `intake/processed/voiceprint-corpus-2026-08-13/`.

## 2026-08-13 — Lock narrator voice and supersede experimental voiceprints

- **Issue:** Which voice guidance should control the novel after the scholastic author corpus and two narrator-revision experiments produced competing or unsuccessful directions.
- **Decision:** Adopt the narrator voice defined in `canon/style-and-voice.md`. Preserve the narrator's grave, restrained intelligence and understated irony. Add life through researched culture, human particularity, relationships, physical experience, pleasure, shame, affection, and scene-level development. Reader attachment arises from patience, fairness, humane attention, and proportion—not narrator punchlines or visible cleverness. Preserve strong sentences unless a specific defect is identified, and reject compression that diminishes possibility, historical breadth, generosity, melancholy, or cadence. The prior bounded scholastic author voiceprint and experimental comparison documents are superseded and removed.
- **Rationale:** Successful tests enriched the Fairweather District and Edgar paragraphs by adding human and cultural context around their strongest existing sentences. Unsuccessful tests replaced understatement with glib compression and narrator performance. The locked voice formalizes the successful method and prevents recurrence of the failed one.
- **Affected files:** `canon/style-and-voice.md`, `skills/novel-writing-team/SKILL.md`, `skills/novel-writing-team/references/writer-prose-continuity.md`, `tests/acceptance-prompts.md`, `tests/fixtures/acceptance-cases.yaml`; superseded files removed from `editorial/proposals/`.

## 2026-08-16 — Place the immersive murder reconstruction after the prosecution case

- **Issue:** Where the novel should present a continuous, real-time reconstruction of Tiebolt's murder while preserving the distinction between dramatic probability and historical proof.
- **Decision:** Add Chapter 15, “The Murder,” immediately after “The Performance of Law.” Resume at the instant Chapter 1 withheld, after Ives comes alongside Tiebolt, and reconstruct the killing from the physical evidence and testimony presented by the prosecution. Run the scene immersively with minimal interruption, but frame it as an assembled account rather than neutral omniscient fact. Move “The Defense Answers” to Chapter 16 and let it immediately restore the absent eyewitness, pressured testimony, disputed statements and alternative possibilities. Renumber all later chapters by one.
- **Rationale:** This placement lets the prosecution's evidence produce its strongest coherent story and gives Chapter 1 a delayed narrative payoff. Placing the defense immediately afterward makes the reader experience the difference between a convincing reconstruction and a provable event rather than receiving that distinction only as abstract commentary.
- **Affected files:** `structure-outline.md`, `research/invention-register.md`, `manuscript/part-05-the-other-side-of-the-coin.md`; future Chapter 15 and Chapter 16 scene contracts and scene-index records when drafted. No manuscript scene prose is approved or changed by this decision.
