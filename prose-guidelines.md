# Prose Guidelines

## Governing rules

- The setting is real.
- The tone is dark.
- The drama is tense.

Write with documentary restraint: concrete physical detail, spare explanation, and no omniscient declaration that justice has been done. Preserve uncertainty wherever the historical record is uncertain.

## Dramatic method

- Let facts change meaning as new information arrives; never manufacture uncertainty or lie to the reader.
- Make danger emerge through procedure, proximity, and consequence—not stock Western signaling.
- Treat the defense seriously. A legitimate challenge to evidence should feel dangerous precisely because it is legitimate.
- Keep violence observational and practical. Ordinary details—cold, rope, horses, distance, fire, weapons—carry more force than melodrama.
- Avoid on-the-nose voiceover, editorial moralizing, and characters explaining the theme.
- Do not turn Sanders into a frontier Atticus Finch. He may be brave and principled, but also ambitious, angry, and capable of believing what he needs to believe.
- Do not treat Tiebolt merely as the narrative excuse for vigilantism.

## Avoid

Do not telegraph sides through glances, smirks, conspicuous hands moving toward pistols, or Ives appearing theatrically relaxed. Avoid phrases such as “no good answer” when the legal answer is clear: if acquitted, Ives goes free, innocent in the eyes of the law.

Prefer the harder physical truth:

> Some men are armed. Actually, everyone is armed. It is not clear who is on what side, but it is clear there are sides.
