## 2026-08-12 — Chapter 1 setting and Tiebolt-Ives account

### Alder Gulch discovery and territorial status

- **Classification:** Documented fact
- **Finding:** Six prospectors discovered placer gold in Alder Gulch on May 26, 1863. Rapid settlement followed. Alder Gulch remained in Idaho Territory until Montana Territory was created in May 1864.
- **Confidence:** High
- **Sources:** Montana Heritage Commission, “History of Alder Gulch”; Thomas J. Dimsdale, *The Vigilantes of Montana* (1866), ch. XII.
- **Limitations/contradictions:** The manuscript's description of fourteen miles, settlement density, and frontier atmosphere combines documented setting with qualitative narration.
- **Manuscript implication:** Chapter 1 must not call the area Montana Territory in December 1863.

### Tiebolt's payment and errand

- **Classification:** Documented in an early partisan account
- **Finding:** Dimsdale reports that Nicholas Tbalt sold a span of mules to Butschy and Clark, received payment, and went to Dempsey's ranch to retrieve the animals.
- **Confidence:** Medium
- **Sources:** Dimsdale, *The Vigilantes of Montana* (1866), ch. XIV.
- **Limitations/contradictions:** Dimsdale wrote to justify the vigilantes and presents contested guilt as certainty. Independent corroboration and the amount of gold have not been established.
- **Manuscript implication:** The scene may state the errand and payment but keeps the quantity qualitative.

### Ives as Tiebolt's killer

- **Classification:** Historical verdict and traditional account; underlying reconstruction disputed
- **Finding:** Ives was tried, convicted, and executed for Tiebolt's murder. Dimsdale attributes the shooting, robbery, and theft of the mules to Ives, drawing partly on testimony that placed Ives going to meet Tiebolt and later possessing the mules.
- **Confidence:** High for accusation, conviction, and execution; medium for the traditional reconstruction; low for undocumented moment-to-moment mechanics.
- **Sources:** Dimsdale, *The Vigilantes of Montana* (1866), ch. XIV; project factual background.
- **Limitations/contradictions:** No surviving witness account consulted for this integration documents a mounted chase or the precise incapacitation shown in Chapter 1.
- **Manuscript implication:** Detailed wounds and killing mechanics are deferred to trial chapters; chase actions are registered as reconstruction.

### Ives and an organized road-agent network

- **Classification:** Disputed claim
- **Finding:** Early vigilante history depicts Ives as a member of an organized band led by Henry Plummer. Later scholarship disputes whether evidence supports the existence of such a highly organized network.
- **Confidence:** High that Ives associated with men later accused as road agents; low that the precise organization described in vigilante narratives existed as claimed.
- **Sources:** Dimsdale, *The Vigilantes of Montana* (1866); Melissa Poindexter Thomasma, “The Lawmen Faced the Outlaws, No Badge Upon a Breast” (University of Montana, 2010).
- **Limitations/contradictions:** Much of the network narrative derives from vigilante accounts and confessions obtained during the hanging campaign.
- **Manuscript implication:** Chapter 1 foreshadows association and later pattern-making while explicitly preserving the historical dispute.

## 2026-08-15 — Nicholas Tiebolt's identity and known past

### Name spellings and reported origin

- **Question:** Which names and origin labels can be attached to the Alder Gulch victim?
- **Classification:** Documented in early accounts; exact identity and birthplace unresolved
- **Finding:** Dimsdale names him Nicholas **Tbalt**. Langford names him Nicholas **Tiebalt** and calls him a “young German.” Dimsdale preserves “the Dutchman” in reported speech about him. The project uses **Nicholas Tiebolt** as its canonical spelling and retains **Nicholas Thiebalt** as a research variant. Diebold and Theobald are search variants only, not established aliases.
- **Confidence:** High for the spellings and labels used by the cited authors; low for any exact European birthplace or nationality inferred from them
- **Sources:** Thomas J. Dimsdale, *The Vigilantes of Montana* (1866), ch. XIV, <https://www.gutenberg.org/files/68146/68146-h/68146-h.htm>; Nathaniel Pitt Langford, *Vigilante Days and Ways* (1890), vol. I, ch. XXIX, <https://readingroo.ms/6/4/1/3/64135/64135-h/64135-h.htm>.
- **Contradictory evidence:** The labels “German” and “Dutchman” are not equivalent evidence of nationality. No birth, baptismal, family, naturalization, or immigration record has been tied securely to the Montana victim.
- **Manuscript implication:** Tiebolt may be described qualitatively as young and German-associated or German-speaking. Do not assign Prussia, a German state, a hometown, family history, immigration date, or native language as fact.
- **Checked:** 2026-08-15

### Washington Gulch, Colorado, around 1861

- **Question:** Can Tiebolt be placed in Colorado before Alder Gulch?
- **Classification:** Author-approved historical premise based on an early partisan account
- **Finding:** Dimsdale reports that, when Tiebolt's body was brought into Nevada, Tom Baume found a knife he had lent Tiebolt in Washington Gulch, Colorado, two years earlier, in the presence of John X. Beidler and William Clark. The author has directed the project to assume this report is true.
- **Confidence:** Medium
- **Sources:** Dimsdale, *The Vigilantes of Montana* (1866), ch. XIV, <https://www.gutenberg.org/files/68146/68146-h/68146-h.htm>.
- **Contradictory evidence:** No independent contemporary record has yet corroborated Tiebolt's presence there. Dimsdale wrote close to the events but openly intended to justify the Vigilantes. The exact Washington Gulch and the length or purpose of Tiebolt's stay are not established.
- **Manuscript implication:** The novel may place Tiebolt in Washington Gulch around 1861 and may use the knife loan to connect Baume, Beidler, Clark, and Tiebolt. It must not present an exact Colorado address, occupation, duration, or direct departure for Alder Gulch without further evidence or explicit invention.
- **Checked:** 2026-08-15

### Nevada City as Tiebolt's probable Alder Gulch base

- **Question:** Where was Tiebolt based immediately before the Dempsey's ranch errand?
- **Classification:** Reasonable inference from an early participant history
- **Finding:** Langford says that Tiebolt was employed by Burtchy and Clark, that nine days passed after he “had left Nevada,” and that he rode a mule from Nevada when he started for Dempsey's. This makes Nevada City his probable immediate working base.
- **Confidence:** Medium-high for departure from Nevada City; medium for residence or working-base status
- **Sources:** Langford, *Vigilante Days and Ways* (1890), vol. I, ch. XXIX, <https://readingroo.ms/6/4/1/3/64135/64135-h/64135-h.htm>.
- **Contradictory evidence:** Leaving a settlement on an errand does not establish legal domicile or length of residence. Dimsdale says the body was brought to Nevada but does not expressly call Nevada Tiebolt's home.
- **Manuscript implication:** The novel may treat Nevada City as Tiebolt's probable working base and point of departure, with qualification. It should not assert that he owned property there or lived there for a specified period.
- **Checked:** 2026-08-15

### Reputation with Butschy/Burtchy and Clark

- **Question:** What personal qualities do early accounts attribute to Tiebolt before his death?
- **Classification:** Documented characterization in early pro-vigilante narratives
- **Finding:** Dimsdale says Tiebolt's employers trusted him implicitly. Langford says he had won their confidence through “fidelity and sobriety.” Their initial belief that he had absconded with the money and mules was presented as painful because it contradicted that established trust.
- **Confidence:** Medium
- **Sources:** Dimsdale, *The Vigilantes of Montana* (1866), ch. XIV; Langford, *Vigilante Days and Ways* (1890), vol. I, ch. XXIX.
- **Contradictory evidence:** Both narratives serve a larger moral account of the crime and the rise of vigilantism; no independent employment record or personal letter has been located.
- **Manuscript implication:** A Tiebolt introduction may humanize him through earned trust, reliability, and sobriety, provided these traits remain attributed to the accounts or dramatized within the invention boundary rather than treated as access to his private character.
- **Checked:** 2026-08-15

### Places connected to the errand but not established as residences

- **Question:** Do Dempsey's ranch, the Pas-sam-a-ri/Stinkingwater Valley, Virginia City, or Bannack count as known Tiebolt residences?
- **Classification:** Documented travel/event associations; residence unsupported
- **Finding:** Dempsey's ranch was Tiebolt's destination. His body was found in the Pas-sam-a-ri or Stinkingwater Valley and brought to Nevada. Virginia City figures in the alarm, community response, and later proceedings. Dempsey's lay on the Virginia City–Bannack road. None of these connections establishes that Tiebolt lived at Dempsey's, in the valley, in Virginia City, or in Bannack.
- **Confidence:** High for the distinction between documented presence or association and unproved residence
- **Sources:** Dimsdale, *The Vigilantes of Montana* (1866), ch. XIV; Langford, *Vigilante Days and Ways* (1890), vol. I, ch. XXIX.
- **Contradictory evidence:** Later summaries sometimes place Tiebolt broadly on the Bannack road, but the early narratives consulted identify Nevada as his departure point and Dempsey's as his destination rather than Bannack as a residence.
- **Manuscript implication:** Use these places for route and event geography only. Do not turn them into biographical residences.
- **Checked:** 2026-08-15

### The two 1846 New York passenger-list candidates

- **Question:** Do either of the two apparent 1846 arrivals identify the later Alder Gulch victim?
- **Classification:** Rejected candidate identifications
- **Finding:** Searches of the New York passenger collection, the 1850 and 1860 censuses, and naturalization-related records did not produce a defensible chain from either arrival to the Montana victim. The strongest follow-up lead for the age-seven, Prussian-born passenger was an 1850 Buffalo household record for Nicholas Theobald, age eleven, born in Germany; that record belongs to a continuing family context and does not connect him to Tiebolt. Inspection of the second apparent candidate showed that the name/surname association depended on manifest indexing rather than a reliable Nicholas Tiebolt entry.
- **Confidence:** High that neither candidate is presently usable as Tiebolt; medium on the precise identity of the first passenger pending a complete genealogical proof standard
- **Sources:** Statue of Liberty–Ellis Island Foundation, New York passenger search, <https://www.statueofliberty.org/discover/heritagesearch/>; FamilySearch, “United States, Census, 1850,” Nicholas Theobald, Buffalo, Erie County, New York, ARK MCYZ-WPK, <https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1:MCYZ-WPK>; FamilySearch searches of the 1850 and 1860 federal censuses and naturalization-related collections.
- **Contradictory evidence:** Similar age, German/Prussian origin, and a phonetically related surname made the first arrival plausible for discovery purposes. No record yet links that person to Colorado, Idaho Territory, Montana, Butschy/Burtchy and Clark, or the 1863 death.
- **Manuscript implication:** Do not assign the Queen of the West arrival, Prussian birthplace, Buffalo childhood, family members, military service, naturalization, or later civil records to Tiebolt. These searches narrow the field but do not supply biography.
- **Checked:** 2026-08-15

## 2026-08-16 — Colorado associates connected to Tiebolt

Detailed search results, candidate lists, negative searches, and links are preserved in `research/tiebolt-colorado-associates.md`.

### John X. Beidler's Colorado-to-Montana path

- **Question:** Can Dimsdale's placement of Beidler in the Washington Gulch episode be independently supported?
- **Classification:** Documented fact for Beidler's movements; Dimsdale's Tiebolt connection remains an author-approved premise
- **Finding:** The 1850 census identifies John Beidler in his Pennsylvania family. The 1861 Colorado territorial election lists J. X. Beidler at Georgia Gulch. Contemporary newspaper reports place X. Beidler operating a pack train between Montgomery City and Denver in January 1862, then describe him in May 1863 as having freighted to California Gulch and Washington Gulch for the preceding year or two before leaving Denver for Bannack.
- **Confidence:** High for Beidler's identity, Colorado freighting, Washington Gulch route, and May 1863 departure toward Bannack
- **Sources:** FamilySearch, 1850 census, ARK M4HH-DTH, <https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1:M4HH-DTH?lang=en>; Denver Public Library, *Colorado 1861 Territorial Election*, <https://history.denverlibrary.org/sites/history/files/Colorado1861TerritorialElection.pdf>; *Rocky Mountain News*, January 17, 1862, <https://www.coloradohistoricnewspapers.org/?a=d&d=RMD18620117-01.2.8>; *Rocky Mountain News*, May 4, 1863, <https://www.coloradohistoricnewspapers.org/?a=d&d=RMD18630504-01.2.11>.
- **Contradictory or missing evidence:** No reliable 1860 census entry was identified. The newspapers do not name Tiebolt, Baume, Clark, or the knife loan.
- **Manuscript implication:** Beidler may be portrayed as an experienced pack-train operator who knew the Colorado mining corridor and traveled toward the Montana diggings in 1863. His route corroborates the setting of Dimsdale's story, not Tiebolt's presence or the knife episode itself.
- **Checked:** 2026-08-16

### Tom Baume's possible Colorado record chain

- **Question:** Can Colorado records identify Dimsdale's Tom Baume before Alder Gulch?
- **Classification:** Reasonable identity hypothesis with a documented competing person
- **Finding:** The 1860 census records Thomas Baume, age twenty-seven and born in England, at Boulder City. The 1861 territorial election lists Thomas Baume at S. S. White's Store. In October 1863 a Denver newspaper identifies a Mr. Baume as D. A. Butterfield's salesman. These may be one man and may be Dimsdale's Tom Baume. However, a separate Indiana-born Thomas Baum lived at nearby Gold Hill in 1860, and a September 1863 coach notice names Tom Baum arriving from Central City.
- **Confidence:** Medium for the English-born Baume as the strongest candidate; low for assigning either 1863 newspaper identity conclusively
- **Sources:** FamilySearch, 1860 census, Thomas Baume, ARK M6RN-G8B, <https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1:M6RN-G8B?lang=en>; FamilySearch, 1860 census, Thomas Baum, ARK M6RN-GSK, <https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1:M6RN-GSK?lang=en>; Denver Public Library, *Colorado 1861 Territorial Election*; *Rocky Mountain News*, September 26, October 3, and October 5, 1863.
- **Contradictory or missing evidence:** No record links the English-born census subject, the voter, the Denver salesman, and the Montana acquaintance through a shared age, birthplace, relative, occupation, or signature. Tax, directory, military, and later naturalization searches produced no identifying record.
- **Manuscript implication:** Use Baume's actions from Dimsdale. Do not assign him English birth, a precise age, Boulder or Central City residence, or Butterfield employment as fact unless the identity chain is strengthened or the author explicitly adopts an invention.
- **Checked:** 2026-08-16

### William Clark candidates

- **Question:** Can Dimsdale's William Clark be distinguished in Colorado records?
- **Classification:** Unresolved candidate set
- **Finding:** The 1861 election contains at least eight W. Clark, Wm. Clark, William Clark, or William Clarke entries across Black Hawk Point, Buckskin Joe, Idaho District, Clear Creek, Nevada City, Oro City/California Gulch, and Plum Creek. The 1862 tax list records a William Clark as a retail dealer at Sacramento Flatts in the California Gulch region. Newspaper records identify one or more Clear Creek men named William Clark but do not connect them to Tiebolt.
- **Confidence:** Low for every candidate identification
- **Sources:** Denver Public Library, *Colorado 1861 Territorial Election*; Denver Public Library, *Territory of Colorado Tax Assessment Lists, 1862–1866*, <https://history.denverlibrary.org/sites/history/files/Colorado_Tax_Assessment_List_1862_1866.pdf>; Colorado Historic Newspapers Collection; USGS, *Historic Trail Map of the Leadville Quadrangle*, <https://pubs.usgs.gov/sim/2004/2820/sim2820.html>.
- **Contradictory or missing evidence:** The name is common, and none of the candidates supplies the age, birthplace, occupation, family, or business link needed to connect him to Nevada City, Montana, Butschy/Burtchy, Baume, Beidler, or Tiebolt.
- **Manuscript implication:** The Sacramento Flatts retail dealer is a useful research lead, not a usable identity. Do not merge the Colorado voters, Clear Creek newspaperman or juror, Sacramento Flatts dealer, Tiebolt's employer Clark, and Dimsdale's William Clark without new evidence.
- **Checked:** 2026-08-16
