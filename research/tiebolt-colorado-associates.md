# Tiebolt's Colorado Associates: Baume, Beidler, and Clark

Research preserved for Chapter IV and later continuity work. This dossier distinguishes records from identity hypotheses. A matching name does not establish that a record belongs to the person described by Dimsdale.

## Narrative anchor

Thomas J. Dimsdale reports that Tom Baume recognized a knife found on Nicholas Tiebolt as one he had lent Tiebolt in Washington Gulch, Colorado, about two years earlier, with J. X. Beidler and William Clark present. Dimsdale also says Elk Morse, William Clark, and Tom Baume arranged Tiebolt's coffin and burial above Nevada City.

- **Classification:** Documented statement in an early pro-vigilante account; author-approved premise for the novel
- **Source:** Thomas J. Dimsdale, *The Vigilantes of Montana* (1866), ch. XIV: <https://www.gutenberg.org/files/68146/68146-h/68146-h.htm>
- **Limitation:** The contemporary Colorado records below corroborate parts of Beidler's movements and produce candidates for Baume and Clark. They do not independently place Tiebolt in Colorado or verify the knife loan.

## Tom Baume

### Documented records and candidates

1. **Thomas Baume in the 1860 census**
   - Age twenty-seven; estimated birth year 1833; birthplace England.
   - Enumerated at Boulder City, Shorter County, Nebraska Territory, then part of the Colorado mining country.
   - FamilySearch record: <https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1:M6RN-G8B?lang=en>
   - **Assessment:** Strong candidate for Dimsdale's Tom Baume because the name is uncommon, the spelling is exact, and the place and date fit. The record alone does not prove identity.

2. **A separate Thomas Baum in the same region in 1860**
   - Age twenty-eight; estimated birth year 1832; birthplace Indiana.
   - Enumerated at the Gold Hill Settlement, Shorter County, Nebraska Territory.
   - FamilySearch record: <https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1:M6RN-GSK?lang=en>
   - **Assessment:** This is a genuine competing person, not merely a spelling variant. It prevents automatic assignment of later `Tom Baum` records to Dimsdale's Baume.

3. **Thomas Baume in the 1861 Colorado territorial election**
   - Listed as `Baume, Thomas : GGG-202` at S. S. White's Store, District 5, Precinct 2, on August 19, 1861.
   - Source: Denver Public Library, *Colorado 1861 Territorial Election*: <https://history.denverlibrary.org/sites/history/files/Colorado1861TerritorialElection.pdf>
   - **Assessment:** Strong evidence that a man using the exact name Thomas Baume remained in Colorado in 1861. Linking him to the English-born 1860 census candidate is a reasonable but unproved identity hypothesis.

4. **Tom Baum arriving from Central City in September 1863**
   - The September 26, 1863, *Rocky Mountain News* coach report lists `Tom Baum` among passengers arriving from Central City on September 24.
   - Source: <https://www.coloradohistoricnewspapers.org/?a=d&d=RMD18630926-01.2.11>
   - **Assessment:** Geographically and chronologically relevant, but ambiguous because the 1860 census establishes both Thomas Baume and Thomas Baum in the mining region.

5. **Mr. Baume working in Denver in October 1863**
   - The October 3 and October 5, 1863, *Rocky Mountain News* identifies a `Mr. Baume` as a popular, knowledgeable salesman for Denver auctioneer and commission merchant D. A. Butterfield.
   - Sources: <https://www.coloradohistoricnewspapers.org/?a=d&d=RMD18631003-01.2.11>; <https://www.coloradohistoricnewspapers.org/?a=d&d=RMD18631005-01.2.13>
   - **Assessment:** The exact surname and date make this a plausible record for Dimsdale's Tom Baume, but no given name appears. If it is the same man, travel from Denver to Alder Gulch before December 1863 was possible but remains undocumented.

### Negative and inconclusive searches

- No convincing Thomas Baume appeared in the 1850 federal census; fuzzy results were unrelated Bone/Bones entries.
- No Baume or defensible Thomas Baume variant appeared in the indexed 1862–66 Colorado territorial tax list.
- No relevant Baume appeared in the *Fiftyniners/Colorado Argonauts* directory.
- No defensible Colorado Civil War service record was identified.
- Searches for Thomas Baume/Baum/Bohm/Bome in indexed Colorado and Montana naturalization collections produced no match. The principal collections begin after the relevant migration period, so this is absence of evidence, not evidence that he never naturalized.

### Working conclusion

- **Classification:** Reasonable identity hypothesis
- **Confidence:** Medium
- **Conclusion:** The best candidate chain is the English-born Thomas Baume in Boulder City in 1860, the exact-name Colorado voter in 1861, and possibly the Denver salesman in October 1863. The separate Indiana-born Thomas Baum requires every `Baum` record to remain unassigned unless another identifier connects it.
- **Book use:** Tom Baume can be used through Dimsdale's documented actions and author-approved Colorado premise. His English birth, age, Boulder residence, Central City travel, and Denver employment must remain qualified unless the identity chain is strengthened.

## John Xavier “X” Beidler

### Documented records

1. **John Beidler in the 1850 census**
   - Age eighteen; born in Pennsylvania; living in Middletown, Dauphin County, with siblings including Jacob, Mary, Samuel, Henry, and George Beidler.
   - FamilySearch record: <https://www.familysearch.org/ark:/61903/1:1:M4HH-DTH?lang=en>
   - The family relationships and attached sources identify him as John Xavier Beidler, born in 1831.
   - **Assessment:** High-confidence identification.

2. **J. X. Beidler in the 1861 Colorado territorial election**
   - Listed as `Beidler, J. X. : JJ-46` at the Gayoso House/Georgia Gulch poll, District 6, on August 19, 1861.
   - Source: <https://history.denverlibrary.org/sites/history/files/Colorado1861TerritorialElection.pdf>
   - **Assessment:** High-confidence exact identification because the initials and surname are distinctive and agree with his later Colorado history.

3. **X. Beidler at Montgomery City in January 1862**
   - A letter dated January 4 and published January 17 reports that X. Beidler was leaving Montgomery City for Denver with his pack train and intended to freight to Montgomery through the winter.
   - Source: *Rocky Mountain News*, “Letter from Montgomery District”: <https://www.coloradohistoricnewspapers.org/?a=d&d=RMD18620117-01.2.8>
   - **Assessment:** Contemporary independent documentation of Beidler's Colorado freighting work.

4. **X. Beidler's Washington Gulch route and departure for Bannack in May 1863**
   - The May 4, 1863, *Rocky Mountain News* describes X. Beidler as an established pack-train operator who had freighted to California Gulch and Washington Gulch during the preceding year or two. It reports that he left Denver that day for Bannock City, the contemporary form used for Bannack.
   - Source: <https://www.coloradohistoricnewspapers.org/?a=d&d=RMD18630504-01.2.11>
   - **Assessment:** High-confidence identification and the strongest independent corroboration found. It places Beidler on the Washington Gulch route during the period of Dimsdale's knife-loan story and documents his 1863 movement toward the Montana goldfields.

### Negative and inconclusive searches

- No reliable 1860 census record for John X. Beidler was identified. A John Beidler in Spring Township, Berks County, Pennsylvania, appears to be another man.
- No Beidler entry appeared in the indexed 1862–66 Colorado tax list or the *Fiftyniners/Colorado Argonauts* directory.
- No defensible Colorado Civil War service entry was identified.
- Naturalization research is inapplicable to Beidler because the 1850 census and later biographical sources establish Pennsylvania birth.

### Working conclusion

- **Classification:** Documented fact
- **Confidence:** High
- **Conclusion:** Beidler's record now forms a coherent path from Pennsylvania in 1850 to Georgia Gulch in 1861, Montgomery District in early 1862, the California Gulch–Washington Gulch freight route, and departure for Bannack in May 1863.
- **Book use:** This path may ground Beidler's knowledge of the Colorado mining corridor, pack-train labor, and migration to the Montana diggings. It corroborates his presence in the setting of Dimsdale's account, not Tiebolt's presence or the knife loan itself.

## William Clark

### Colorado candidates

The name is too common to identify Dimsdale's William Clark without an age, birthplace, middle initial, occupation, family member, or business associate.

1. **1861 Colorado territorial election candidates**
   - `Clark, Wm. : C-250` — Black Hawk Point, District 4, Precinct 1.
   - `Clark, Wm. : G-212` — Buckskin Joe's, District 7, Precinct 6.
   - `Clark, W. : NN-1` — Idaho District, District 3, Precinct 6.
   - `Clarke, W. H. : P-64` — Clear Creek Cherokee Crossing.
   - `Clarke, Wm. : WW-436` — Nevada City, Colorado, District 5, Precinct 1.
   - `Clark, W. : XX-454` and `XX-464` — Oro City/California Gulch, District 7, Precinct 8.
   - `Clark, Wm. : YY-25` — Plum Creek/Coberly's House.
   - Source: <https://history.denverlibrary.org/sites/history/files/Colorado1861TerritorialElection.pdf>

2. **William Clark at Sacramento Flatts in the 1862 tax list**
   - Residence: Sacramento Flatts; occupation/license: retail dealer; September 1862; Division 11.
   - Source: Denver Public Library, *Territory of Colorado Tax Assessment Lists, 1862–1866*: <https://history.denverlibrary.org/sites/history/files/Colorado_Tax_Assessment_List_1862_1866.pdf>
   - Sacramento Flats was associated with Sacramento City and the California Gulch mining district. USGS historical mapping places the Sacramento district in the middle portion of California Gulch: <https://pubs.usgs.gov/sim/2004/2820/sim2820.html>
   - **Assessment:** This is the strongest contextual candidate because Beidler freighted between California Gulch and Washington Gulch, and a retail dealer could plausibly employ men or trade in livestock. The connection is circumstantial and must not be stated as identity.

3. **Clear Creek newspaper records**
   - A William Clark served on the jury in the January 1860 Chicago Creek hanging case.
   - A recurring `Wm. Clark` was associated with Clear Creek in 1860 newspaper reports, including mining news supplied to *The Western Mountaineer*.
   - Source example: <https://www.coloradohistoricnewspapers.org/?a=d&d=WMN18600125.2.15>
   - **Assessment:** These records may concern the Black Hawk/Clear Creek voter rather than the man connected to Washington Gulch. They do not supply a Tiebolt link.

4. **Other weak records**
   - Denver advertised-letter lists include William or Wm. Clark in 1861 and 1863.
   - The *Fiftyniners/Colorado Argonauts* directory lists the Chicago Creek juror and a separate William H. Clark, born in Ohio in 1835 and resident in the region from 1858. Neither can be assigned to Dimsdale's Clark.

### Working conclusion

- **Classification:** Unresolved candidate set
- **Confidence:** Low for every individual identification
- **Conclusion:** The Sacramento Flatts retail dealer is the most narratively relevant working candidate, followed by the Oro City/California Gulch voters. No record yet identifies Dimsdale's William Clark.
- **Book use:** Use only Dimsdale's reported actions—that Clark witnessed the knife loan and helped bury Tiebolt—and the early accounts of Tiebolt's employment by Butschy/Burtchy and Clark. Do not merge those Clark references, assign a Colorado residence or occupation, or identify him with the Sacramento Flatts dealer without new evidence or an explicit authorial invention.

## Remaining proof targets

- A directory, employment, probate, mining-claim, court, tax, naturalization, obituary, or family record that links the English-born Thomas Baume to the Montana Tom Baume.
- A source naming D. A. Butterfield's salesman as Thomas or Tom Baume.
- Evidence that distinguishes the two 1860 men, Thomas Baume and Thomas Baum, after 1861.
- A Montana record giving William Clark an age, birthplace, middle initial, occupation, partner, family member, or prior Colorado camp.
- Business, livestock, freighting, or mule-purchase records connecting William Clark to Sacramento Flats, Washington Gulch, Nevada City, Butschy/Burtchy, or Tiebolt.
- Independent evidence for Tiebolt himself in Colorado. Beidler's documented route strengthens the setting but does not prove Tiebolt's presence.
