# Opening Scene Outline — The Murder

Treat the opening as subjective reconstruction, not a clean omniscient account. The hook is speed, landscape, money, fear, and uncertainty about exactly what happened.

**Opening image:** Hoofbeats carrying across open country before the riders are visible.

## Beat 1 — Flight

Tiebolt is riding hard. Not traveling fast—fleeing. He looks back repeatedly. The horse is tiring.

## Beat 2 — The country

The landscape is enormous and almost indifferent to him: mountains, open distance, winter light, sky occupying most of the world. There is nowhere to hide.

## Beat 3 — What he carries

Tiebolt has gold on him. Not a fortune by the standards of Alder Gulch, but perhaps everything by his own: the next season, the next claim, the next version of his life.

## Beat 4 — The second rider

Another horse appears behind him. George Ives. Do not yet explain who he is, why he is pursuing Tiebolt, or what happened before the chase began.

## Beat 5 — Closing distance

Tiebolt keeps pushing his horse. Ives gains. One horse is faster, fresher, or better ridden.

## Beat 6 — Gold becomes weight

The gold that represented possibility minutes earlier has become the reason someone may be trying to kill him.

## Beat 7 — No authority

There is no town in sight. No sheriff. No witness. No one coming over the ridge. Whatever happens between these men will happen because one of them is stronger.

## Beat 8 — The encounter

Ives reaches him. Keep the precise mechanics restrained unless firmly supported by the historical record. Let any dismount, demand, struggle, shot, or blow emerge from the best-supported reconstruction rather than invented choreography.

## Beat 9 — Silence

The chase ends abruptly. After the noise of the horses, the landscape becomes quiet again.

**Closing image:** Tiebolt lies alone in a country large enough to conceal almost anything. His gold is gone. Cut away before explaining who George Ives is.
